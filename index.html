1. Introduction

This project was conducted to design the first original content for 'StreamKUS'. Rather than relying on the production team's fleeting intuition, we aim to build a robust, universally appealing format based on deeply empirical insights.

2. Data Collection Process & Extracted Detailed Data

  • Data Source Justification: User reviews and ratings from the global review platform IMDb. While platforms like YouTube or Twitter provide immediate reactions, they are often fragmented, highly sensationalized, and lack structured episode-by-episode granularity. IMDb was selected as the primary source because it offers a centralized, highly structured, and historically deep repository of long-term critical viewer feedback, allowing for consistent time-series and episode-level sentiment tracking.
  • Selected Programs: Hell's Kitchen, America's Got Talent, and Survivor.
  • Selection Criteria: Targeted long-running programs with a minimum of 100 reviews and at least 50 total episodes (spanning at least 5 seasons) to ensure statistical reliability and to capture the "enduring success formula" rather than a one-off hit.
  • Total Analyzed Corpus: 1,070 valid episode-level text reviews after data cleaning (Survivor: 888, America's Got Talent: 118, Hell's Kitchen: 64).

3. Text Mining Methods & Analysis Scripts

Data Preprocessing

The raw `.xlsx` data underwent extensive preprocessing. We standardized sentiment labels, converting numeric values (-1, 0, 1) into definitive categorical data (Negative, Neutral, Positive). We deliberately stripped extraneous numerical variables such as user 'Ratings' and temporal markers like 'Seasons' during the text analysis phase. The rationale was to isolate and extract universal behavioral themes and structural program flaws that transcend specific season popularity.

Sentiment Processing & Topic Modeling (BERTopic & Codex)

To move beyond simplistic word clouds, we deployed a BERT-based (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers) model for contextual sentiment analysis at the sentence level. We combined this with OpenAI Codex to extract coherent topic clusters. Each extracted keyword was then mathematically mapped against the sentiment polarity of its source review, allowing us to generate precise percentage correlations (e.g., proving that 41.2% of all negative reviews in Hell's Kitchen specifically referenced 'Incompetence').

Automated Web Scraping & Data Structuring

This script was utilized to bypass standard pagination limits and directly query the review platform's GraphQL API. It automatically extracted thousands of user reviews, including text, ratings, and timestamps, across multiple seasons and episodes. The gathered raw data was structured into dataframes, forming the foundational dataset for our subsequent sentiment and topic modeling analyses.

4. Findings (Data Visualization)

Logical Connection: From Sentiment Ratios to Deep Topic Extraction

The initial pie charts below provided the critical starting point for our analysis. By revealing significant negative sentiment volumes—specifically in Survivor (46.6%) and Hell's Kitchen (26.6%)—we recognized that simply copying these formats would inherit their structural flaws. We then utilized topic extraction strictly on these negative segments to pinpoint exact failure mechanisms, and conversely on the positive segments to find universal drivers of engagement.

Core Insights Extracted via Topic Modeling:

  • The Positive Drivers (Skill and Psychological Realism): Viewers overwhelmingly praised unscripted, high-stakes competence. Hell's Kitchen's 'professional chef competitions (48.6%)' and Survivor's 'pure tribal council dynamics (57.4%)' emerged as the strongest positive correlations.
  • The Negative Drivers (Incompetence and Production Artificiality): The data heavily penalized structural manipulation. The strongest negative correlations were Survivor's 'excessive producer intervention/twists (33.6%)' and Hell's Kitchen's 'unskilled cast (41.2%)'.

5. Content Proposals (Integrated Ecosystem)

Rather than merely listing these insights, we propose 'The Professional' as an integrated survival ecosystem. By combining the proven success elements while structurally eliminating the failure points, we create a program where dramatic tension arises naturally from the participants rather than artificial direction.

Participant Composition (Elimination of Incompetence)

Addressing the major flaw in Hell's Kitchen (41.2% negative feedback regarding incompetent cast members), our show will strictly recruit seasoned professionals with over 10 years of industry experience. This ensures "flawless performances by the best," aiming for the 95.1% high satisfaction rate observed in AGT.

Autonomous System (Blocking Production Intervention)

To directly counter the highest source of dissatisfaction in Survivor (33.6% negative feedback for excessive producer intervention), the authority to eliminate participants will be delegated entirely to the contestants themselves through an autonomous voting council. This induces a pure strategic battle free from artificial manipulation.

Subdued Production (Focusing on the Essence)

Incorporating feedback on AGT's 'excessive audience reactions' (41.4%) and 'forced melodrama' (20.7%), we will eliminate emotional editing. The program will adopt a documentary-style realism that focuses squarely on the technical craftsmanship and strategic moves of the professionals.

Symbolism (Providing Catharsis)

Mirroring Hell's Kitchen's success factor, the 'black jacket' (31.4% positive), we will award specially crafted uniforms and tools to survivors. This provides visual catharsis and reinforces the program's core theme of professional authority.

6. Conclusion

Summary of Core Findings and Proposals

This project empirically demonstrated the success formulas and failure factors of survival programs by analyzing 1,070 text reviews from IMDb. The analysis revealed that viewers responded highly positively to overwhelming professionalism and sophisticated participant strategies, while showing strong aversion to participant incompetence and artificial production interventions. Based on these findings, we propose a new content model, 'The Professional', an integrated survival ecosystem that combines the recruitment of seasoned experts with an autonomous council system to reflect the core empirical needs of the audience.

Limitations of the Analysis

There were practical limitations during the research process. First, due to a lack of detailed reviews for specific individual episodes, we had to adopt an integrated program-level analysis rather than the initially planned episode-specific sentiment tracking. Second, utilizing data solely from the single platform IMDb introduces the possibility that the results are skewed toward the tendencies of a specific Western-centric user base.

Future Directions

It is necessary to collect additional data from YouTube trending features and social media platforms to dynamically reflect real-time public reactions. Furthermore, a comparative analysis with popular domestic programs like 'Culinary Class Wars' could help derive localized strategies tailored to different cultural contexts. Ultimately, introducing episode-level time-series sentiment analysis to track text characteristics at specific viewership fluctuation points will provide more concrete, data-driven production guidelines.
